User-learned + research-confirmed (2026) doctrine. Governs every build, rewrite, and the win_audit tool.

**One page serves ONE intent.** Mixing informational + transactional on one page satisfies neither and ranks for neither (intent mismatch is the #1 reason technically-fine pages don't rank). Four intents: informational (learn), navigational (find a site), commercial (compare), transactional (act — includes local "near me / emergency / walk-in / book").

**Service pages = transactional + local intent.** Their target query family is "ER near me / emergency room white rock / walk in ER for stomach pain dallas" — NOT "when to go to ER for abdominal pain / what causes stomach pain" (those are informational). A service page must NOT chase informational queries.

**Above the fold = what they came for.** For emergency/local: click-to-call `tel:` button, availability badge (Open 24/7 · Walk In · No appointment · ~ETA), crisis-validating H1, one reassurance line, address + Get Directions — all in the first section. Phone in the title tag ~ +209% conversion; 67% of emergency searches convert within 1 hour. Keep the transactional core tight; do not bury it under education.

**Informational depth → blog spokes, not the service page.** Hub-and-spoke: the "when to go / causes / what to expect" content becomes a blog post (spoke) that internally links to the service page. Light trust/education layer on the service page is fine (intent stacking) but sits BELOW the transactional fold and links out — never tries to out-article the articles.

**One primary keyword, many rankings.** A well-built page naturally ranks for hundreds of related queries; don't force unrelated/wrong-intent keywords onto it.

**AI Overviews angle:** informational "what is / how / why / when should" queries trigger AIO ~99.9% (Know category) and eat the click; transactional/local/visit-in-person rarely trigger AIO. So targeting the transactional-local intent on service pages is also AIO-safer.

**How to find the intent for a query:** search it, look at what format Google already ranks (articles vs service/product pages) — that IS the intent verdict. Match the page type to it.

## Page-robustness layers beyond intent (CRO + extraction + YMYL) — research-confirmed 2026

Intent match is the foundation; these are the levers that make a page actually convert and get cited. Apply to every page/post; bake into page_quality_gate + builder globally.

**1. Message match (highest-leverage CRO variable).** The H1 must echo the searcher's query almost verbatim. Query→ad→H1 alignment is the single biggest conversion lever; a clear, specific, benefit/crisis-framed H1 beats a clever one. Reinforce the same intent/message consistently down the page ("scent" — don't change the subject mid-page).

**2. Answer-first / inverted pyramid.** Lead with the answer in the first sentence, then detail, then context. Treat every H2 as its own mini inverted pyramid (sub-question answered in sentence 1). Concise 40-60 word passages, question headings, lists, tables, definitions = exactly what featured snippets AND AI Overviews extract. Never bury the answer under a long intro.

**2a. H2 standard (user-flagged 2026-06-15) — PROPER, COMPLETE, AI-QUERY-SHAPED H2s.** People increasingly search by asking questions to AI, which extracts content passage-by-passage. So every content H2 must be a complete natural-language heading that mirrors how someone actually asks it — ideally a full question ("When is a head injury an emergency?", "What will it cost?") — never a vague fragment ("Over Time", "Our Services", "Comprehensive Care"). The section's first sentence directly answers that H2 (40-60 words). This makes each section independently citable by answer engines. Not EVERY H2 must be a literal question, but each must be complete and self-explanatory; favor question form for the ones users actually search as questions. Retrofit fragment H2s on existing pages when touched.

**3. ONE primary CTA per page (Hick's law / choice overload).** Multiple competing CTAs split attention and cut conversion 15-30%; a single primary action can lift clicks dramatically. One solid primary CTA per viewport; everything else secondary/outline. Too many buttons/links/options reduce conversion. (Aligns with design system: one solid CTA per viewport.)

**4. Social-proof placement.** Put trust signals near/below the CTA and near forms, not dumped in one block. CTA placed immediately AFTER 4.5★+ reviews converts ~33% better; logos/testimonials directly under the CTA lift conversion ~59%. Pick 2-3 highest-leverage signals per page. (ER constraint: org-attributed only — ACEP/AHA/CDC, real reviews; no fabricated proof, no provider bylines until consent.)

**5. YMYL / E-E-A-T (mandatory for these health sites — neither the video nor generic CRO covers this).** Medical = YMYL → E-E-A-T is the entry requirement, not a bonus. Needs: verifiable publisher identity + consistent NAP; every medical claim sourced to CDC/WHO/FDA/PubMed/NIH consensus; Medical* schema (EmergencyService/MedicalClinic/Physician/MedicalProcedure) — boosts AIO/snippet citation. This is WHY a freestanding-ER service page legitimately carries a light trust/education layer that a locksmith page would not — but it still sits BELOW the transactional fold and links informational depth to spokes.

**6. Page experience / mobile (table stakes).** LCP < 2.5s (test on 4G), good INP, no layout shift; click-to-call tel: button, tap targets ≥44px, contact info above the fold on mobile. Emergency/local is mobile-dominant.

**7. Friction + measurement.** Remove above-the-fold friction (no walls of intro, minimal form fields ≤7). Diagnose with behavioral tools (Microsoft Clarity / session recordings) — they reveal what GA misses. NOTE: Clarity is an external install we can't queue via the plugin; flag it to the operator rather than assume it exists.

Source video (corroborated): Edward Sturm "Match Search Intent or Lose the Click" (2026-06-12) — intent match, clear H1, split info/commercial, answer fast, remove friction above fold, internal links as next step, social proof placement, intent repetition, Clarity. Independent research confirmed all + added the YMYL/E-E-A-T layer and the single-CTA/message-match magnitudes.
