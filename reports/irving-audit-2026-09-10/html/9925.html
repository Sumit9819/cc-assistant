How Botox Works

Botox is the brand name for onabotulinumtoxinA, a purified neuromodulator FDA-approved for cosmetic use since 2002. Micro-injections temporarily block signals between nerve and muscle, allowing the overlying skin to smooth out.

Phase 01

The Mechanism

Targets one muscle at a time

Botulinum toxin type A blocks acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction. The muscle relaxes; the wrinkle softens. Surrounding expression stays intact. NCBI clinical reference.

Phase 02

Onset and Duration

Visible by day 10, lasts 3-4 months

Initial smoothing begins around day 3 to 4. Full effect appears by day 10 to 14. Most patients see 3 to 4 months of result before the muscle gradually regains function.

Phase 03

Safety Profile

23+ years of clinical use

With more than two decades of cosmetic use, Botox has one of the most extensively studied safety profiles in aesthetic medicine. Side effects are rare and typically mild when administered by a trained medical practitioner, consistent with American Academy of Dermatology safety guidance.

Subtle muscle activity begins to reappear, especially in highly expressive patients. Some dynamic movement returns before any visible lines. Time to schedule your next consultation.

Month 3 to 4

Time to Re-Treat

Most patients schedule re-treatment in this window. Treating before complete return often means future treatments need fewer units, because treated muscles gradually weaken with consistent disuse.

Common Misconceptions

Botox Myths vs Clinical Facts

Six misconceptions we field at every Irving consultation, answered with the clinical references behind each correction.

Myth

"Botox is dangerous because it's a toxin."

Fact

Botulinum toxin type A used cosmetically is purified, FDA-cleared since 2002, and administered in micro-doses (typically 10 to 30 units per area). Each unit contains nanogram-level quantities of active protein, far below any threshold for systemic concern when administered by a trained clinician.

Source: NCBI StatPearls clinical reference
Myth

"Botox will make my face look frozen or expressionless."

Fact

Frozen results come from over-dosing or imprecise injection placement, not from Botox itself. Anatomy-mapped dosing by a Board Certified clinician produces selective muscle relaxation while preserving natural expression. Our 2-week follow-up exists specifically to catch any asymmetry early.

Source: American Academy of Dermatology guidance
Myth

"If I stop Botox, my wrinkles will come back worse than before."

Fact

Stopping Botox returns muscles to baseline function. Wrinkles return gradually over 3 to 4 months, not worse than they were originally. Long-term consistent use can actually reduce baseline static lines because treated muscles atrophy slightly with disuse.

Source: NCBI clinical review on neuromodulator pharmacology
Myth

"Botox is only for older women."

Fact

The average first-time cosmetic neurotoxin patient is 35 years old. Preventative or "baby" Botox in the late 20s is increasingly common and trains muscles before deep static lines form. Men account for roughly 7% of treatments and that share is growing yearly.

Source: AAFPRS Annual Census
Myth

"More units mean better results."

Fact

Optimal results come from matching dose to specific muscle strength, not from maximizing units. Most forehead patients need 10 to 20 units, glabellar 15 to 25, crow's feet 10 to 15 per side. Over-dosing produces frozen expressions, not better smoothing.

Source: FDA Botox prescribing information
Myth

"Telehealth Botox is the same as in-clinic."

Fact

Botox requires an in-person injection by a trained clinician. No legitimate telehealth provider ships Botox for self-injection because the procedure requires anatomy mapping, sterile administration, and immediate clinician oversight for adverse events. Any service claiming otherwise is operating outside FDA-cleared protocols.

Source: AAD patient safety guidance

Botox Aftercare

Follow these guidelines for the first 24 hours to protect your results and minimize bruising.

✓

DO

  • Stay upright for at least 4 hours
  • Gently exercise treated muscles by smiling and frowning
  • Drink plenty of water
  • Apply cool compress if mild swelling appears
  • Sleep on your back the first night
  • Return for your complimentary 2-week review
✕

DON'T

  • Lie down or bend over for 4 hours after
  • Touch, rub, or massage treatment areas for 24 hours
  • Do vigorous exercise for 24 hours
  • Drink alcohol for 24 hours
  • Get facials, microneedling, or saunas for 1 week
  • Take blood thinners for 24 hours unless prescribed

Frequently Asked Questions About Botox

Common questions answered by our clinical team.

How many units of Botox will I need?
Most adult patients need 10 to 20 units per treatment area. Forehead lines typically use 10 to 20 units; glabellar "11" lines use 15 to 25 units; crow's feet use 10 to 15 units per side. The exact dose depends on your facial anatomy, muscle strength, and aesthetic goals, assessed during your consultation.
How long does Botox last?
Botox typically lasts 3 to 4 months in most patients. First-time patients sometimes see slightly shorter duration as the muscle adjusts. With consistent quarterly treatments, many patients experience longer-lasting results over time as treated muscles weaken with disuse.
Does Botox hurt?
Most patients describe the sensation as a brief pinch, similar to a mosquito bite, at each injection point. The full treatment takes 10 to 15 minutes. Topical numbing cream is available on request for sensitive patients or larger treatment areas.
When will I see results?
Initial smoothing begins around day 3 to 4. Full results appear by day 10 to 14 as the neuromodulator binds with motor neurons and reduces muscle activity. We schedule a complimentary 2-week follow-up to assess and touch up any asymmetry if needed.
Who administers the Botox?
All injectable treatments at Irving Health and Wellness Clinic are administered by Lori Secerovic, APRN, FNP-C, a Board-Certified Advanced Practice Registered Nurse with formal aesthetic injectable training. View her full credentials.
What should I avoid before and after?
For 24 hours before: skip blood thinners (aspirin, ibuprofen), alcohol, fish oil, and vitamin E. For 4 hours after: avoid bending over or vigorous exercise. Avoid touching, rubbing, or applying pressure to the treatment areas for 24 hours. Your full aftercare instructions are provided at your appointment.
