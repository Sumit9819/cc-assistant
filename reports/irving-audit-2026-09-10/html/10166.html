Who Semaglutide Is Not For

An honest program names its limits before taking your money. Here is exactly where we draw the line.

We will not prescribe it if

  • You or a close family member have had medullary thyroid cancer
  • You have MEN 2 (multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2)
  • You are pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive
  • You have had a serious allergic reaction to semaglutide

We screen more closely first if

  • You have a history of pancreatitis
  • You have active gallbladder disease
  • You have diabetic retinopathy
  • You take insulin or a sulfonylurea, which raises hypoglycemia risk

This is not a form you fill out and forget. Lori reviews your bloodwork and history personally before any prescription is written. And being ruled out is not a dead end: if the whole GLP-1 class is off the table for you, our wellness coaching program builds a medication-free plan around nutrition, movement, and your labs. If semaglutide simply is not the right fit within the class, tirzepatide may be evaluated instead. Full label details are on MedlinePlus.

Semaglutide Questions, Answered

Do I need bloodwork before starting semaglutide?

Yes. Thyroid, fasting insulin, HbA1c, and hormone panels come before any prescription. The workup rules semaglutide in or out, sets your starting dose, and screens for the contraindications listed on this page. It also gives us the baseline your monthly progress is measured against.

How much weight can I lose on semaglutide?

In the 72-week head-to-head trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine, semaglutide users lost 13.7% of their body weight on average, roughly 33 pounds. Individual results depend on your dose, labs, and habits, which is why we track body composition monthly rather than promising a number.

How much does semaglutide cost in Irving?

Our plans are flat-rate: $499 for one month, $1,137 for three months, or $2,094 for six months, with monitoring included. There are no hidden dispensing fees. Insurance coverage for weight loss varies widely, and our team checks your options during the consultation.

What are the common side effects?

Nausea is the most common, reported by roughly 44% of trial participants, along with abdominal discomfort in about a quarter. Most digestive effects appear during dose increases and settle as your body adjusts. Slow, supervised titration is the main tool for keeping them manageable.

Should I choose semaglutide or tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide produced larger average weight loss in the head-to-head trial, but cost, insurance coverage, and your own response matter just as much. Our semaglutide vs tirzepatide comparison walks through the trial data, and your consultation settles the choice with your labs on the table.
