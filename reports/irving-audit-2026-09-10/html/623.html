Understanding Your Hormonal Health

It can be incredibly frustrating to be told your labs are normal when you still do not feel like yourself.

For men, who often experience low testosterone and brain fog, standard testosterone ranges have shifted significantly over time.

For women navigating perimenopause relief options, the natural transitions of menopause can bring disruptive symptoms that are frequently overlooked. We look beyond basic lab averages to truly listen to your symptoms and optimize your hormones to a level where you can thrive.

What Are the Signs of Hormonal Imbalance?

Identify the signs of hormonal changes early so we can support your journey back to wellness.

Your Journey to Restored Vitality

We believe in a collaborative, data driven approach to your care.

The Lab Draw

We start with a full hormone panel that covers your entire cascade. The draw includes total and free testosterone, estradiol, progesterone, and DHEA-S. We also test TSH, Free T3, Free T4, Reverse T3, TPO, TgA, AM cortisol, SHBG, fasting insulin, and HbA1c.

Clinical Analysis

Your markers are reviewed against optimal physiological ranges, not just standard lab reference ranges. For example, total testosterone at 350 ng/dL falls inside the conventional reference (264 to 916) but well below the optimal target of 700 to 900 ng/dL for an active man. That gap is why some patients are told they are normal while still feeling far from it.

Your Custom Protocol

You receive a custom bioidentical prescription tailored to your labs, symptoms, and lifestyle. Delivery options include subcutaneous pellets every 3 to 4 months, intramuscular injections weekly or biweekly, daily topical creams, or sublingual troches. We choose the method based on your absorption profile, schedule, and adherence preferences.

Ongoing Optimization

Retesting is scheduled every 3 months in the first year, then every 6 months once your levels stabilize. Each cycle reviews CBC, a comprehensive metabolic panel, lipid panel, PSA for men over 40, and ferritin. This catches early shifts in hematocrit, liver enzymes, or prostate signals before they become a problem.

Compare Hormone Delivery Methods

Four delivery methods. Each fits a different lifestyle, monitoring need, and dose precision target. Your protocol can mix methods over time as your goals shift.

Pellets

Subcutaneous pellet inserted in the hip every 3 to 4 months. Steady release with no daily action. Best for travelers, needle-averse patients, and anyone who has missed pills before.

Injections

Weekly self-administered intramuscular shot, typically into the thigh or glute. Most precise dose control and the lowest cost per cycle. Best for patients comfortable with needles who want easy adjustments.

Topical Cream

Compounded cream applied daily to inner arm or thigh skin. Easy to adjust dose by changing the amount applied. Avoid skin-to-skin contact with kids and partners for 4 hours after each dose.

Sublingual Troche

Compounded lozenge that dissolves under the tongue once or twice daily. Bypasses first-pass liver metabolism. Good fallback when injectables and creams do not suit your lifestyle.

What to Expect Week by Week

Improvement is not linear. Sleep and mood often shift first as cortisol and progesterone rebalance. Energy and libido follow during weeks three through six. Body composition changes arrive last, typically around month four. Onset timing varies by baseline labs, delivery method, and adherence to your dosing schedule.

Week 1 to 2

First Signals. Initial energy and mood lift in some patients. Sleep quality often improves first as cortisol and progesterone rebalance. No body composition changes yet.

Week 3 to 6

Building. Mental clarity and libido begin to shift. Hot flashes start to ease for menopausal patients. Strength feels easier in the gym. Brain fog lifts.

Month 2 to 3

Lab Recheck. First quarterly retest of all 14 markers. Symptom log review with your APRN. Most patients have their dose dialed in by this point. Dose adjustments are common.

Month 4 to 6

Sustained Change. Body composition begins to shift. Lean mass increase, fat loss in stubborn areas. Workout recovery improves. Stable steady-state energy and mood.

What is the difference between TRT and testosterone optimization therapy?

TRT (testosterone replacement therapy) targets clinically low testosterone, typically below 300 ng/dL on conventional labs, with the goal of pushing levels back into the normal reference range.

Testosterone optimization therapy targets the upper third of the reference range (600 to 900 ng/dL for men), where most patients feel their best even when conventional labs read normal. Both are medically supervised. The goal differs.

Are bioidentical hormones safer than synthetic hormones?

Bioidentical hormones share the exact molecular structure of what your body produces, while synthetic versions are close approximations.

The 2002 Women’s Health Initiative cardiovascular concerns were tied to synthetic conjugated equine estrogens plus medroxyprogesterone, not to bioidentical estradiol and progesterone. We use FDA-recognized bioidentical compounds compounded under USP 795 and USP 797 sterility standards, with dosing matched to your own lab values.

How long until I feel results from bioidentical HRT?

Most patients notice better sleep and mood within 2 weeks. Energy and libido shift between weeks 3 and 6.

Body composition changes (lean mass increase, fat loss in stubborn areas) typically begin around month 4. Onset varies with baseline hormone levels, delivery method, and adherence.

Can I do hormone therapy while on Mounjaro, Wegovy, or Zepbound?

Yes, and many of our patients combine the two. GLP-1 medications and bioidentical hormone therapy target different systems and do not interact pharmacologically.

Hormone optimization often improves the muscle-preservation outcomes of GLP-1 therapy because testosterone and estradiol support lean tissue retention during caloric restriction. We coordinate dosing schedules and quarterly labs across both protocols at your consultation.

Is hormone replacement covered by insurance in Texas?

Bioidentical pellet therapy is rarely covered by Texas insurance plans because it is considered elective. Lab work is often partially covered, so we bill labs separately and your insurance applies to your bloodwork directly.

We provide itemized superbills for HSA and FSA reimbursement on consultation and lab fees. Some patients also use medical FSA dollars for the pellet insertion procedure itself.
