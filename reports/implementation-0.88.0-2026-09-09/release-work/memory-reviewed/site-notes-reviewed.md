- Local audit scores and fixed citation, word or snippet quotas are not Google requirements. Support consequential claims with suitable evidence and context. Low CTR alone cannot establish why performance changed. Compare captures and investigate competing explanations.

- ## Content drafting + optimization rules (apply to every new post/page AND every existing-post optimization)

1. **Niche and demand research**: use current services, audience questions, existing coverage and available GSC observations. GSC is optional evidence; absent queries do not mean no opportunity. Never fabricate demand.

2. **Comparable-page research**: inspect actual relevant competitor pages and primary sources. Record URLs, capture dates and coverage; do not call them top-ranking without observed search evidence. Develop a supported useful contribution without copying or claiming global novelty.

3. **User-first alongside keyword/competitor**: the post serves the reader first. **These rules apply universally**: new blog posts, new pages, optimization of existing posts, optimization of existing pages.
- ## Paragraph breaks follow MEANING, not sentence counts (REVISES the older "2-3 sentence max" rule)

The earlier "2-3 sentences max per paragraph" guidance was a crude band-aid for wall-of-text paste-ins from Google Docs. It is NOT the actual writing principle. From 2026-05-13 forward use the meaning-based rule below for every new post/page AND every optimization of an existing post/page:

- **Break the paragraph when**: the idea shifts, the argument pivots, the path or direction of the prose changes, you want a sentence to be remembered (emphasis), or you set up a contrast that needs to land.
- **Stay together when**: every sentence develops the same point and breaking would lose the thread. Forcing a break mid-argument creates choppy, robotic prose.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs are GREAT** when the sentence carries weight on its own. Example: *"Real drowning is silent."* deserves its own line. *"We also have on-site CT."* does not. Use single-sentence paragraphs for punchlines, pivots, contrasts, or memorability anchors that the reader should walk away with.
- **4-5 sentence paragraphs are FINE** when every sentence develops the same point and they truly need each other. A coherent argument step does not need to be artificially broken.
- The standard is flow + emphasis + meaning, not sentence count. NYT, The Atlantic, and patient-facing NEJM material all follow this. So do we.

This applies to every word the cc-assistant tool writes or edits on this site: new blog posts, new pages, optimization of existing posts, optimization of existing pages, and meta/title/description copy.
