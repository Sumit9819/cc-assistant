EMERGENCY TRIAGE

Is This an Emergency? When Chest Pain Means Call 911 Now

The American Heart Association and ACEP classify these warning signs as immediately life-threatening. If you notice any of them, call 911 right away. Do not drive yourself.

EMS can start aspirin, oxygen, and a 12-lead EKG before you reach the ER. They alert our team to your arrival so triage begins the moment you walk in.

Pressure or tightness > 15 minutes

Classic ischemic chest discomfort that doesn't ease with rest. AHA.

Pain radiating to jaw, arm, neck, or back

Referred pain is a hallmark of myocardial infarction. AHA.

Sudden severe shortness of breath

May indicate pulmonary embolism, MI, or aortic dissection. ACEP.

Cold sweat with chest pain

Autonomic response with high predictive value for heart attack. AHA.

Light-headedness, dizziness, or fainting

Possible arrhythmia or reduced cardiac output. AHA.

Weakness or numbness on one side of body

Stroke can present with chest pain in atypical cases. CDC.

Call 911 immediately if you experience any of these signs. Don’t drive yourself. EMS can start treatment on the way to the ER.

CONDITIONS WE EVALUATE

What Causes Chest Pain? Six Conditions We Rule In or Out

Not every chest pain is a heart attack, but our team rules out the dangerous cardiovascular causes first. The differential below runs in order of urgency, starting with conditions that become fatal within minutes if missed.

Blood clots in the lung and aortic dissection can both present as chest pain. Stroke-related pain in the neck or jaw also triggers the same triage protocol.

Heart Attack (STEMI / NSTEMI)

12-lead EKG within 5 minutes plus hs-troponin in triage flag a heart attack. STEMI activates the cath lab immediately. AHA.

Unstable Angina

Chest pain at rest or with minimal exertion, even with negative troponin, signals high-risk coronary disease. HEART score guides admission. AHA.

Pulmonary Embolism

Sudden shortness of breath with chest pain. We use Wells score, D-dimer, and CT pulmonary angiogram to rule it in or out. ACEP.

Aortic Dissection

Sudden tearing chest or back pain. CT angiogram of the chest rules it in or out. Time-critical. ACEP.

Pericarditis & Myocarditis

Sharp positional pain, EKG changes, and bedside echo identify pericardial inflammation. NIH.

Non-Cardiac Causes

GERD, musculoskeletal strain, and anxiety can mimic cardiac symptoms. We rule out heart causes first, every time. AHA.

RISK FACTORS

Who Is at Highest Risk for Cardiac Chest Pain?

Knowing your risk changes how we work up your chest pain. The more factors that apply, the lower our threshold for advanced cardiac testing like serial troponin or stress imaging.

Roughly half of U.S. adults have hypertension per CDC data, and a third have abnormal cholesterol. Chest pain in the over-40 group with even one risk factor gets the full workup by default.

ATYPICAL PRESENTATIONS

Why Does Chest Pain Look Different in Women, Diabetics, and Older Adults?

Up to 38% of women, 25% of people with diabetes, and nearly half of older adults can have a heart attack without classic chest pressure. MedlinePlus documents jaw pain, back pain between the shoulder blades, sudden fatigue, and nausea as common atypical presentations. Recognizing these patterns at triage is what prevents a missed MI in the very groups most likely to have one.

WOMEN

Classic chest pressure may be absent. We watch for:

  • Nausea or indigestion
  • Unusual fatigue (often days before)
  • Shortness of breath
  • Jaw, back, or neck pain
  • Cold sweat without chest pressure

Source: AHA Go Red for Women / NEJM

PEOPLE WITH DIABETES

Autonomic neuropathy can blunt pain. We watch for:

  • Shortness of breath without obvious pain
  • Sudden severe fatigue or weakness
  • Hypotension or light-headedness
  • Mild upper-abdominal discomfort
  • New unexplained sweating

Source: ADA / AHA

ADULTS 65+

Atypical presentation is common at older ages:

  • New confusion or altered mental state
  • Syncope or unexplained fall
  • Shortness of breath without chest pain
  • Sudden severe weakness
  • Abdominal pain mimicking GI illness

Source: ACC / NHLBI

OUR WORKUP PROCESS

What We Look For in Your Chest Pain Workup

Time matters. Every step of the workup below is timed to ACC and ACEP standards.

We deliver a 12-lead EKG within 5 minutes and hs-troponin during triage rather than after wait. On-site CT imaging and lab are available if the picture is unclear. The goal: rule in or rule out the highest-risk conditions before you sit down.

01

TRIAGE · 0–2 MIN

Brief Triage and Vitals

Pulse, BP, SpO2, focused history, and IV access primed. Chief complaint logged so the cath lab can be on standby if STEMI activates downstream. ACEP.

02

EKG · ≤ 5 MIN · ACC STANDARD

12-Lead EKG

Read at the bedside by an ER physician. ST-elevation activates the cath lab immediately. ACC door-to-EKG target is 10 minutes; our internal target is 5.

03

TROPONIN · 5–10 MIN

High-Sensitivity Troponin

First hs-troponin drawn at triage. If positive or rising, NSTEMI workup begins immediately. Repeat at 1 and 3 hours per ESC 0/1/3 algorithm.

04

IMAGING · 15–30 MIN

Chest X-Ray and Targeted CT

Chest X-ray for every chest pain. CT pulmonary angiogram if PE suspected (Wells score). CT angiogram of chest if aortic dissection suspected. ACEP.

05

DECISION · 30–60 MIN

HEART Score and Disposition

HEART score combines history, EKG, age, risk factors, and troponin. Score ≥7 admits. 4 to 6 observation. 0 to 3 home with cardiology follow-up. AHA/ACC pathway.

WHERE TO GO FOR CHEST PAIN

Should I Go to the ER, Urgent Care, or Cardiologist for Chest Pain?

Chest pain is one of the few symptoms where venue choice can change outcome. Urgent care cannot run hs-troponin or interpret an EKG against ACC criteria. A cardiologist’s office cannot admit you if the EKG looks bad.

Only an emergency room can do all three: rule out infarction in real time, stabilize, and bridge to admission or discharge with a confirmed answer.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Common Questions About Chest Pain and the ER Workup

The eight questions below answer the most-searched chest-pain queries with the same clinical detail we use at the bedside. Each answer is brief by design so a reader can grab the headline in 10 seconds, and longer if you tap to expand.

If your specific question is not covered, walk in or call (469) 943-2939 and ask the on-shift physician directly.

How long does a chest pain workup take in the ER?

A standard chest pain workup at ER of White Rock takes 30 to 60 minutes from arrival to disposition. Triage and 12-lead EKG happen in the first 5 minutes. Troponin draws and imaging follow. The HEART score guides whether you go home, observe, or admit. ACEP.

What does an EKG check for?

A 12-lead EKG records the heart’s electrical activity from 12 angles. ER physicians read it at the bedside to detect ST-elevation (active heart attack), arrhythmias, conduction blocks, and signs of previous MI. STEMI on EKG triggers immediate cath lab activation. ACC.

What is troponin and why is it important?

Troponin is a protein released by injured heart muscle. High-sensitivity troponin can detect a heart attack within 1 to 3 hours of symptom onset. A negative high-sensitivity troponin at 0 and 3 hours can safely rule out MI in most patients. ESC.

Can stress or anxiety cause chest pain that feels like a heart attack?

Yes. Panic and severe anxiety can produce chest tightness, shortness of breath, and even arm numbness that mimics a heart attack. We rule out cardiac causes first with EKG and troponin before attributing pain to anxiety. AHA.

Is chest pain always a sign of a heart attack?

No. Common non-cardiac chest pain causes include GERD, musculoskeletal strain, pleurisy, and anxiety. However, only an ER workup can rule out cardiac causes with certainty. ACEP guidance is to evaluate every chest pain in the ER first. ACEP.

How long after a heart attack does troponin show up in blood?

High-sensitivity troponin can be detected within 1 to 3 hours of MI onset. Older troponin assays took 6 to 12 hours. ER of White Rock uses high-sensitivity assays so we can rule out MI faster, often within 3 hours of arrival. ESC.

When should I worry about chest pain that comes and goes?

Intermittent chest pain that recurs over hours or days, especially with exertion, can signal unstable angina, a warning sign before a heart attack. Do not wait for the pain to become constant. Come in for evaluation. AHA.

Can heartburn feel like a heart attack?

Yes. GERD and heart attacks can present with similar burning chest pressure. The safest path is to come in and let an EKG and troponin rule out cardiac causes. Antacids relieving the pain does not confirm GERD over a heart attack. AHA.

INSURANCE & COVERAGE

Insurance & Patient Protections

You are protected by two federal laws when you come to a freestanding ER for chest pain. These protections apply even if you are uninsured or out-of-network.

EMTALA guarantees screening and stabilization regardless of ability to pay. The No Surprises Act caps out-of-network charges to your in-network deductible and copay.

EMTALA PROTECTION

Federal law requires every ER to medically screen and stabilize you for an emergency condition before any insurance question. Applies even if you are uninsured.

NO SURPRISES ACT

For emergency care, you pay no more than your in-network cost-sharing even when the facility or provider is out-of-network.

COMMERCIAL PLANS ACCEPTED

We work with most major commercial insurance plans. Call our billing team to verify in-network status before non-emergency visits.
