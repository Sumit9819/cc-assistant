When is a concussion an emergency?

Most concussions are not life-threatening, but the same hit can cause bleeding or swelling that looks like a concussion at first. Go to the ER right away, or call 911, if you notice any of these after a blow to the head. They can signal a more serious head injury, not just a concussion:

  • Loss of consciousness, even briefly
  • A headache that keeps getting worse
  • Repeated vomiting
  • Slurred speech, weakness, or numbness
  • One pupil larger than the other
  • A seizure, or trouble waking up
  • Confusion or agitation that grows

When in doubt, get checked. Sources: CDC HEADS UP and CDC TBI, accessed June 2026.

What does a concussion feel like?

You do not need to black out to have a concussion. Symptoms fall into four groups and can start right away or show up hours later.

Physical

Headache, dizziness, nausea, balance trouble, blurry vision, or sensitivity to light and noise.

Thinking

Feeling foggy or slowed down, trouble concentrating, or answering questions more slowly than usual.

Mood

Feeling irritable, sad, nervous, or more emotional than normal after the injury.

Sleep

Sleeping more or less than usual, or trouble falling asleep in the days after the hit.

How long does a concussion take to heal?

Most people feel better within a couple of weeks. Rest your brain early, then ease back in stages so symptoms do not return.

First 24 to 48 hours

Rest your brain and body. Go easy on screens, bright light, and intense activity while symptoms are strong.

Days to two weeks

Most symptoms fade. Slowly add light activity as long as it does not bring the symptoms back.

If it lasts longer

Symptoms beyond two to four weeks (post-concussion syndrome) deserve a re-check to rule out other causes.

Never risk a second hit

A second concussion before the first heals can cause serious, lasting harm, especially in children and athletes.

Hit your head during sports, a fall, or a crash?

Do not wait to see if it passes. A quick check rules out bleeding and starts your recovery on the right foot.

What happens when you arrive for a concussion check?

No appointments and no referral loops. Here is the path from door to answers.

1. Rapid triage

A nurse reviews how the injury happened and checks your vitals and neuro signs within minutes of arrival.

2. CT if needed

A head CT on site rules out bleeding or swelling when your symptoms or history call for imaging.

3. Concussion assessment

We test memory, balance, and concentration to confirm a concussion and gauge its severity.

4. Recovery plan

You leave with clear rest guidance, warning signs to watch, and steps for a safe return to activity.

Safe return to school, work, and sports

Going back too soon is the most common reason symptoms drag on. Step up only when each stage stays symptom-free.

Brain rest first

Short on screens and demanding tasks for the first day or two, then add light mental activity gradually.

Ease back into school or work

Return part-time and build up. Pull back a step if symptoms flare with effort.

Get cleared before contact sports

Athletes should be symptom-free and medically cleared before returning to contact play.

Concussion questions, answered

How do I know if I have a concussion?

A concussion is likely if you feel dazed, foggy, dizzy, or have a headache after a hit to the head, even without blacking out. We evaluate every head injury and tell you whether it is a concussion or something more serious.

Do I need a CT scan for a concussion?

Not always. CT looks for bleeding, not the concussion itself. We scan when your symptoms, age, or history suggest a risk of bleeding, and we explain the decision either way.

Is it safe to sleep after a concussion?

Yes, for most people sleep is fine and helps recovery. What matters is being able to wake the person normally. We tell you the specific warning signs that mean come back right away.

How long until I can return to sports?

Most athletes need at least one to two symptom-free weeks plus a gradual return, and should be medically cleared before contact play. Children and teens take longer and need extra caution.

Can concussion symptoms show up later?

Yes. Symptoms can appear hours after the injury, and some last for weeks. Come back if symptoms worsen or new ones appear.

Do you treat children with concussions?

Yes. Our clinicians treat children of all ages, every hour of the day, and watch for the symptoms that kids cannot always describe.
