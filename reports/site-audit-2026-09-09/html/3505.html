WHEN TO COME IN

When Is This an ER Visit?

When you are not sure where to go, this is the fast rule of thumb. And minutes matter. The CDC reports stroke patients who arrive within 3 hours of first symptoms often have less disability 3 months after the stroke.

Call 911 Now

Not breathing, severe bleeding, unresponsive, or signs of stroke or heart attack. Do not drive yourself.

Come to the ER

Chest pain, head injury, severe abdominal pain, high fever, broken bones, or trouble breathing.

Urgent Care or Primary

Minor colds, mild rashes, routine prescription refills, or non-urgent follow-up visits.

ER VS URGENT CARE

What Can the ER Do That Urgent Care Cannot?

A full emergency room runs CT, X-ray, ultrasound, and lab tests on site, treats life-threatening illness and injury, and evaluates every patient regardless of insurance. Urgent care handles minor illness and small injuries during limited hours. When symptoms are sudden or severe, the emergency room is the safer choice.

Emergency Room

  • Chest pain, stroke signs, trouble breathing, severe bleeding
  • CT, X-ray, ultrasound, and lab results during your visit
  • Open 24/7, every day, no appointment needed
  • Every patient evaluated under EMTALA, insured or not

Urgent Care

  • Colds, mild rashes, minor cuts, and sprains
  • Limited imaging and lab capability on site
  • Daytime and evening hours, closed overnight
  • A good fit when your doctor cannot see you today

“The emergency department is the best option for concerning symptoms, severe illness or injury.”

American College of Emergency Physicians, 2023
