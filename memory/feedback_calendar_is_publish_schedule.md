Never draft or queue calendar posts ahead of their scheduled week. Work the calendar week by week, drafting each batch 1-3 days before its publish dates.

**Why:** 2026-07-27, irvingwellnessclinic. After Week 1 (Aug 3-7) was fully authored, I rolled straight into drafting Week 2's melasma post (scheduled Aug 10) on July 27 - two weeks early. Operator: "why are you creating week 2 blog right now?" The failure compounds because this operator approves pendings within minutes, so a queued draft is effectively a published post. Week 1's four posts had already gone live the same day; approving the melasma draft would have made five posts published on one date, collapsing the 4-per-week cadence into a bulk dump.

**Why the cadence matters:** (1) publishing velocity - a one-day burst then weeks of silence reads as bulk content, the spaced schedule reads as a living site; (2) measurement - each post's success_metrics judges clicks from a zero baseline, and simultaneous publication muddies attribution; (3) the calendar IS the deliverable the operator asked for - front-running it defeats its purpose.

**How to apply:**
1. Draft a week's posts no earlier than ~3 days before the first publish date of that week.
2. If a draft lands early anyway, tell the operator to HOLD it in the Pending inbox until its date, or approve it with the WordPress publish date edited to the scheduled date so WP schedules instead of publishing immediately.
3. Between publishing weeks, the right work is everything else: checkpoints, measurement reads, GBP, schema verification, refreshes of existing posts - not next week's content.
4. "Keep going" / fast approvals from the operator are momentum on the CURRENT scope, not authorization to advance the schedule. See [[feedback_scope_one_thing_at_a_time]].
