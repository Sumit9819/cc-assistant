User's content-strategy direction (2026-07-16, during eroflufkin blog work): they value (a) external links to authoritative sites and (b) quoted professionals with name + profession in blog posts.

**Why:** Matches the evidence base — Princeton GEO study: quotes/stats lift AI visibility up to ~40% (directional); named source + number + date beats vague "studies show". Quotes also add net-new content competitors lack.

**How to apply:**
- External links: authority means .gov/.edu/medical consensus (NIDDK, MedlinePlus, NWS, OSHA, CDC), NOT "big websites" generally. Verify URLs resolve before queueing (CDC/NHTSA 403-block fetchers; cite by name only if unverifiable).
- Quantity (user, 2026-07-16): minimum 2 quotes per post when 2 verifiable ones exist; 1 is fine otherwise. Style quotes as branded pull-quote cards and the Medically-Reviewed byline as a matching chip (specs in each site's memory Rules).
- Quotes: ONLY previously published statements the person actually said. Verify VERBATIM by fetching the source page; attribute with exact name + credential + role at the time of the statement; link the source. Good hunting grounds: ACEP/AMA press releases, university medical center newsrooms (.edu), agency advisories.
- REJECTED approach (user, 2026-07-16, eroflufkin #457): drafting a candidate quote for the provider to sign off. Never queue words a person has not said, even gated on approval. If the site's own provider has real published/recorded statements, those are ideal; otherwise quote national bodies.
- No competitor clinic names/entities in quotes or bios ([[no-competitor-clinics-in-bios]]).
- Pairs with the provider byline rollout; both blocked on provider sign-off per site.
- Relevance bar (user, 2026-07-16): the quote must speak to the post's SPECIFIC subject, not generic clinical/professional process (Delgado "clinical picture" quote rejected as not resonating).

**TIGHTENED 2026-07-27 (user, irvingwellnessclinic 6356).** The relevance bar above was too soft to act on: I read it and still proposed a generic industry-commentary quote. A candidate quote must now pass ALL FIVE gates, and the burden is on the quote to earn its place:

1. **Subject gate.** The quote names the post's actual subject: the condition, the treatment, the mechanism, or the patient group. Commentary about the *industry*, *marketing*, *the science* in general, or *how clinicians think* fails, however famous the speaker.
2. **Fact-transfer gate (the decisive one).** The quote must carry a number, a mechanism, a threshold, a contraindication, or a decision criterion. **Test: delete the quote. If the reader loses no fact, it fails.** Rhetoric, vibes, and directional opinion are not facts.
3. **Expertise-match gate.** The speaker's specialty matches the specific claim (endocrinologist on hormones, hematologist on B12, dermatologist on scarring). A general "senior editor" or "professor of medicine" commenting outside their specialty fails.
4. **Verification gate.** Verbatim, fetched from the source page, exact name + credential + role at time of statement, live source link. Never paraphrase into quotation marks.
5. **Commercial-sanity gate.** Never quote a flat denial of a service the site sells ("there's no point to X") when no substantive rebuttal exists. A critique the clinic can legitimately agree with AND answer with its own protocol is acceptable ONLY if it also clears gates 1 and 2.

**Worked rejection (2026-07-27):** Robert Shmerling MD, Harvard Health, "The marketing has gotten way out ahead of the science," proposed for post 6356 (IV therapy for fatigue). Fails gate 1 (about marketing, not IV therapy or fatigue), gate 2 (no transferable fact), gate 3 (Senior Faculty Editor, not a fatigue or nutrition specialist). Correct move when no quote clears all five: **ship the cited statistics alone.** A missing quote costs a little info_gain; a hollow quote costs credibility. Never fill the slot just because the slot exists.
