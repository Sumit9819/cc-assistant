When writing or refreshing blog content that synthesizes from external authoritative sources (CDC, NIH, MedlinePlus, AHA, peer-reviewed journals), the value-add must come from NEW DATA and INSIGHTS, not opinions. This positions the blog as a curator-with-evidence rather than an aggregator parroting other sites.

**REQUIRED — every blog post must add at least one of:**
1. A new statistic or data point with cited authoritative source (e.g., "A 2026 NIH PMC study of 1,200 patients found...")
2. A comparison/contrast across multiple sources not present in any single original (e.g., "While the CDC recommends X, the AHA's 2025 update suggests Y under conditions Z")
3. An updated 2025/2026 finding that supersedes older common-knowledge claims
4. A structural insight from reading multiple sources (e.g., "Of the 7 published RCTs on tirzepatide, 5 used 6-month follow-up; only 2 extended past 12 months")
5. A specific clinical-protocol bridge — how the data informs the actual SKU offering ("Because IV bioavailability is 100% vs ~30% oral, the Myers Cocktail dose at this clinic delivers...")

**FORBIDDEN — opinions framed as personal stance:**
1. "We believe / we think / in our experience" applied to medical/scientific claims (this is allowed only when describing clinic-specific protocols the user has authority over)
2. "The best / the only / definitively" without a cited source
3. Recommendation framing that implies the writer is the authority ("You should…" sans citation)
4. Causal claims beyond what the evidence supports

**Acceptable third-person framing:**
- "Clinical research shows…"
- "A 2026 NIH study found…"
- "The AHA's 2025 guidelines recommend…"
- "Compared with [other protocol], [this protocol] shows X% improvement in [outcome] over [timeframe], per [source]"
- Clinic-bound statements (facts about service, not opinions on science): "Our clinic uses bioidentical pellets" or "The Myers' Cocktail dose at this clinic is X"

**Source priority order:**
1. Primary studies — NIH PMC, peer-reviewed journals (NEJM, JAMA, etc.)
2. Clinical guidelines — CDC, AHA, NHLBI, ASAPS, AACE, FDA, NIDDK
3. Authoritative reference — MedlinePlus, NHS, NCBI Bookshelf
4. DO NOT CITE — Healthline, WebMD lifestyle, Verywell, magazine aggregators, AI-generated content sites

**Why:** 2026-05-12 — User explained: opinions belong to people who are the primary source of information. Since blog content aggregates from external sources, the writer doesn't have authority to opine on the science. The differentiator must be data quality and synthesis depth, not voice or stance. This pairs with [[feedback_content_quality_and_research]] (zero plagiarism + external research) and [[feedback_niche_search_discipline]] (only in-niche topics).

**How to apply:**
1. Before drafting, identify the 1-3 NEW data points the post will add beyond what's on the source sites.
2. Source each data point from priority-1 or priority-2 above. Cite inline.
3. After drafting, scan for any first-person voice on medical/scientific claims and rewrite to third-person attributed framing.
4. Clinic-bound voice ("at our clinic") is fine for protocol/SKU facts but not for science claims.
5. If the post has no new data and is purely a synthesis of common knowledge, it shouldn't be published — pick a different angle.
