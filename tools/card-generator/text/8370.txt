## What is Dermaplaning and How Does it Work?

Dermaplaning is a minimally invasive cosmetic procedure that involves a healthcare professional or aesthetician gently scraping the surface of your skin with a sterile surgical scalpel. While the word "scalpel" might sound intimidating, the process is actually quite soothing and painless.

### The Science of Exfoliation

The primary goal of dermaplaning is to remove the top layer of dead skin cells (the stratum corneum) along with fine vellus hair, commonly known as "peach fuzz." According to NIH, ur skin naturally regenerates itself, but as we age, that natural shedding process slows down. When dead cells linger on the surface, they can clog pores, highlight fine lines, and leave the complexion looking dull.

By manually removing this debris, dermaplaning triggers the skin's signaling pathways to focus on repair and renewal. According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), controlled exfoliation can improve skin texture and tone by promoting a more organized epidermal structure.

## The Top Benefits of Dermaplaning for Your Skin

Why is everyone talking about this treatment? It's because the results are often visible the moment you walk out of the clinic.

### Achieving a Flawless Makeup Application

If you've ever applied foundation only to find it clinging to tiny hairs or dry patches, you know how frustrating it can be. By removing the vellus hair and smoothing the skin's surface, dermaplaning creates a perfectly level "canvas." Makeup glides on effortlessly and sits closer to the skin, providing that "airbrushed" finish without the need for heavy filters.

### Better Absorption of Skincare Products

You invest a lot of time and money into your serums and moisturizers. However, if those products are sitting on top of a layer of dead skin, they can't do their jobs effectively. Once the barrier of dead cells is removed, your medical-grade skincare products can penetrate deeper into the epidermis, making your at-home routine much more effective.

## Is Dermaplaning Safe? What You Need to Know

Safety is our top priority. For most people, dermaplaning is exceptionally safe. However, it emphasizes that any procedure involving the "resurfacing" of the skin should be approached with care, particularly regarding the tools used.

### Professional vs. At-Home Dermaplaning

While "eyebrow razors" are sold in drugstores for home use, they are not the same as a professional treatment. A clinical setting ensures:

- Sterility: We use medical-grade, single-use blades to prevent infection.
 
- Precision: Professionals are trained in the exact angle and pressure needed to exfoliate without causing nicks or irritation.
 
- Assessment: We can determine if your skin is currently too sensitive, for instance, if you have an active acne flare-up or inflammatory condition, before proceeding.

## What to Expect During Your Appointment

We want you to feel relaxed from the moment you sit in the chair. A typical session lasts about 30 to 45 minutes.

- Cleansing: We start with a gentle cleanser to remove oils and impurities.
 
- The Procedure: Your provider will hold your skin taut and use short, light strokes with the blade at a 45-degree angle. Most patients describe the sensation as a light "tickle" or a gentle scratching.
 
- Hydration: We finish by applying a soothing serum and a broad-spectrum sunscreen.

## Aftercare: Protecting Your New Glow

Because dermaplaning removes the outermost protective layer of dead skin, your "fresh" skin is more vulnerable to environmental damage. The CDC reminds us that UV radiation can damage skin cells even on cloudy days.

After your treatment, you must:

- Wear Sunscreen: Use an SPF of 30 or higher daily.
 
- Avoid Harsh Retinols: Give your skin 48–72 hours before using strong acids or retinoids.
 
- Hydrate: Drink plenty of water and use a gentle moisturizer to support the skin barrier.

## FAQs

### Will my hair grow back thicker or darker?

This is a common concern, but it is a myth. Dermaplaning targets vellus hair (peach fuzz), which is different from terminal hair (like the hair on your head). Removing it does not change the biological structure of the hair follicle. 

### How often should I get dermaplaning done? 

To maintain results, most patients schedule a visit every 4 to 6 weeks. 

### Does dermaplaning hurt? 

Not at all! It is a non-invasive treatment. Most patients find it quite relaxing. If you have extremely sensitive skin, let your provider know so they can adjust their technique.

### Can I get dermaplaning if I have acne? 

If you have an active, cystic acne breakout, we recommend waiting until the skin has healed. Running a blade over an active blemish can cause irritation or spread bacteria. 

### Is there any downtime? 

There is zero "downtime." You might experience slight redness for an hour or two, but you can return to your normal activities and apply makeup immediately.

### Is dermaplaning better than a chemical peel? 

They serve different purposes. Dermaplaning is a physical exfoliation, while peels are chemical.
