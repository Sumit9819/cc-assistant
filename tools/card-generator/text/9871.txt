## Why Picking the Right Clinic Matters

A poor choice costs more than money. The wrong clinic delays real progress, prescribes medications without testing, skips the maintenance phase, and the rebound weight gain hits harder than the original loss.

The opposite is also true. A solid medical weight loss program can produce sustainable results that diet apps and gyms cannot.

## Question 1: Are the Providers Board-Certified?

Weight loss is medicine. Hormones, insulin, and metabolic markers all get adjusted along the way. The clinician running your plan should be a board-certified MD, DO, or NP, not a coach with a certificate from a weekend course.

Verify board certification on the state medical board website. Any real clinic will list provider credentials publicly.
"The best weight loss programs are the academic programs or community programs attached to a medical center run by obesity medicine specialists that offer lifestyle medications, psychology and metabolic surgery options."
Dr. Caroline Apovian, professor of medicine, Harvard Medical School | TODAY

The principle scales down: medical oversight first, marketing second.

## Question 2: Does the Program Start with Bloodwork?

Any clinic that prescribes GLP-1 or Lipo-B without first running a thyroid panel, fasting insulin, HbA1c, and a hormone panel is treating symptoms, not causes. Your body may be holding weight because of insulin resistance, low thyroid, perimenopause, or chronic cortisol. The medication that actually helps depends on which one applies.

Our explainer on hormones and weight gain walks through the most common metabolic drivers and the labs that catch them.

## Question 3: Is the GLP-1 FDA-Approved or Compounded?

Compounded GLP-1 medications are not the same product as Ozempic, Mounjaro, or Wegovy. Compounded versions often use different active forms, different concentrations, and different fillers. Some patients tolerate them well. Others do not.

If a clinic offers GLP-1 at a price point that feels too good to be true, it is almost certainly compounded. Ask directly. The answer should be in writing.

The FDA publishes safety alerts on compounded semaglutide. Read those before signing up.
"Any legitimate medical weight loss program today should offer FDA-approved, brand-name GLP-1 therapies."
Dr. Christopher McGowan, gastroenterologist and obesity medicine specialist | TODAY

## Question 4: Is Pricing Transparent or Hidden?

Run a mental stopwatch on this one. A real clinic gives you a flat monthly cost in the first conversation. A red-flag clinic hides pricing behind a free consultation that turns into a sales pitch. We hold ourselves to this test: our consultations are free for every patient, and program pricing is stated before you commit to anything.

Specifically ask:

- What is the monthly program cost?
 
- What does that include (visits, medication, follow-up)?
 
- What costs extra (bloodwork, body composition scans, supplements)?
 
- Is there a contract or month-to-month billing?
 
- What is the cancellation policy?

## Question 5: Is There a Written Maintenance Plan?

Most programs market the loss phase. Few talk about what happens after. Ask the clinic exactly what their maintenance protocol looks like:

- How are doses tapered?
 
- What happens when the medication stops?
 
- What lifestyle anchors does the program build before you exit?
 
- How often do you check in during maintenance?

If a clinic shrugs at the maintenance question, the program is built to lose you weight and then lose you as a patient. Our breakdown of long-term maintenance habits covers what real maintenance looks like.

## Question 6: How Are Results Measured?

Scale weight is a noisy signal. Real medical programs track:

- Body composition (lean mass vs fat mass)
 
- Waist circumference
 
- Resting metabolic rate
 
- Bloodwork progress (insulin, HbA1c, lipids)

If the only number a clinic celebrates is the scale, expect rebound. Muscle loss disguised as weight loss is the most common failure mode of mass-market programs.

## Question 7: How Is the Program Different from a Diet?

This is the litmus question. A real clinic should be able to answer in 30 seconds, with specifics. Diets target calories. Medical weight loss targets the metabolic blocks that make calorie restriction fail.

If the answer is "we just give you GLP-1," it is not a medical program. If the answer is "we test, we treat the cause, and we adjust," that is the real thing. Our piece on medical weight loss vs dieting covers the full distinction.

## Red Flags to Walk Away From

Skip any clinic that does any of these:

- Promises a specific number ("Lose 30 lbs in 30 days").
 
- Charges for the consultation without telling you the program cost first.
 
- Refuses to run bloodwork before prescribing.
 
- Pushes a long-term contract.
 
- Uses generic stock photos with zero provider credentials on the site.

The CDC recommends 1 to 2 pounds per week as the safe, sustainable rate. Anyone promising more is selling fluid loss as fat loss.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it worth paying for a medical weight loss program?

For patients with metabolic drivers (insulin resistance, thyroid issues, hormonal shifts, PCOS, perimenopause), yes. Diet alone usually fails when the body has a medical reason to hold weight. For patients without those drivers, a structured fitness coach or app may be enough.

### How much should a real weight loss program cost?

Bloodwork-only programs run $150 to $300 per month. Programs with prescriptions usually fall between $200 and $600 per month, plus the cost of the medication. Anything substantially cheaper usually skips the medical component.

### Should I trust online-only weight loss programs?

Online programs work for follow-up visits, but the initial workup benefits from in-person bloodwork and body composition. Hybrid programs (in-person intake, virtual follow-ups) usually outperform fully remote programs for stubborn weight.

### What questions should I ask in the first consultation?

Use the 7 questions above. Add: Will I see the same provider every visit? Who do I contact if a medication causes side effects? What happens if I plateau?

### How do I choose the right clinic for GLP-1 weight loss treatment?

Apply the same seven questions, with two extra filters: the clinic should run labs before prescribing, and it should offer FDA-approved products rather than compounded copies. Then ask which medication fits your case and why. Our semaglutide vs tirzepatide comparison shows the trial data a good clinic will walk you through.

### How long should I stay on GLP-1 medication?

Most patients stay on a maintenance dose long-term. Stopping the medication entirely often leads to weight regain. A real clinic builds your taper and maintenance plan from day one.
