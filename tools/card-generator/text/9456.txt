## Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide: The Short Answer

Key takeaway: In the first head-to-head trial, tirzepatide produced greater average weight loss than semaglutide over 72 weeks. Both medications work well, and their side-effect rates were nearly identical. The right choice usually comes down to cost, insurance coverage, availability, and how your body responds.

Semaglutide (sold as Wegovy and Ozempic, and regulated by the FDA) and tirzepatide (sold as Zepbound and Mounjaro) are the two leading prescription options in medical weight loss. For years, patients had to compare them across separate studies. Now there is a direct answer.

## What the First Head-to-Head Trial Found

The SURMOUNT-5 trial followed 751 adults with obesity, but without diabetes, for 72 weeks. Each person took either tirzepatide or semaglutide at the highest dose they could tolerate. Results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine in May 2025.

Tirzepatide users lost 20.2% of their body weight on average, roughly 50 pounds. Semaglutide users lost 13.7%, roughly 33 pounds. Nearly one in three tirzepatide users lost at least a quarter of their body weight, compared with one in six on semaglutide.

## How These Medications Work (and How They Differ)

Both drugs imitate GLP-1, a gut hormone released after you eat. They quiet appetite signals in the brain, slow how fast your stomach empties, and improve how your body handles insulin. The practical effect: you feel satisfied with less food, without white-knuckle willpower.

The difference is that tirzepatide activates a second hormone receptor called GIP alongside GLP-1. That dual action appears to amplify the metabolic effect, which is the leading explanation for its stronger trial results. Neither drug is a shortcut; both work best alongside real changes to metabolic health, nutrition, and movement.

## Which One Is Right for You?

The trial average is not a personal guarantee. In practice, we weigh four things with every patient.

- Coverage and cost: Insurance formularies often prefer one drug over the other. The best medication is frequently the one you can stay on consistently.

- Your target: If a larger total reduction matters for your health goals, the tirzepatide data is hard to ignore.

- Prior response: Some patients plateau on one medication and respond well after switching. Non-response to one drug does not mean both will fail.

- Tolerability: Side-effect rates were nearly identical in the trial, so comfort differences are individual, not drug-wide.

## Side Effects: Closer Than You Might Expect

SURMOUNT-5 undercut a common assumption: the stronger drug was not harsher. About 44% of participants in both groups reported nausea, and about 25% in both groups reported abdominal pain. Most digestive effects appear during dose increases and ease as the body adjusts.

Clinically supervised dosing matters here. We titrate slowly, adjust when symptoms appear, and screen for the conditions that rule these medications out. For complete safety profiles, MedlinePlus maintains plain-language pages on semaglutide and tirzepatide, and the NIH's NIDDK overview explains who these drugs are for.

## FAQs

### Is tirzepatide stronger than semaglutide?

For average weight loss, yes. In the 72-week head-to-head trial, tirzepatide produced 20.2% average body-weight reduction versus 13.7% for semaglutide, and twice the share of participants reached a 25% reduction. Individual results vary widely, and some people respond better to semaglutide than the averages suggest.

### Can you switch from semaglutide to tirzepatide?

Yes, switching is common in clinical practice. It is usually considered when weight loss plateaus, side effects persist, or insurance coverage changes. The transition is dose-managed by your provider rather than started at the top dose, which keeps digestive side effects predictable during the change.

### Why do results differ so much between people?

Appetite and weight are regulated by overlapping hormone pathways, and no two metabolisms match. Genetics, insulin resistance, sleep, medications, and hormone conditions all shift how well a GLP-1 drug works. That is why we run labs first and track response instead of assuming the trial average applies to you.

### Do the results last after stopping?

Weight regain is common after stopping either medication, especially without habit changes. Studies of both drugs show partial regain within a year for many patients. A maintenance plan, whether a lower dose, habit systems, or both, should be part of the conversation before you ever start.
