## IV Therapy 101: What It Is (and Isn’t)

The basics. Intravenous (IV) therapy delivers fluids, vitamins, minerals, and other nutrients directly into your bloodstream. This allows for predictable hydration and targeted nutrient support under clinical supervision.

Benefits vs. limits. IV therapy can support hydration, help replenish certain nutrients, and complement a healthy lifestyle. It is not a cure-all and shouldn’t replace medical care for illness or emergencies.

Who it may suit. Many people explore IV therapy for general wellness, travel recovery, athletic recovery, or immune support, always after a quick clinical screen.

When to pause or avoid, be sure to tell your clinician if you’re pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney or heart conditions, G6PD deficiency (relevant for high-dose vitamin C), have allergies to ingredients, or are feeling unwell with fever/infection. Transparency helps us keep you safe.

## 48–24 Hours Before Your Appointment: Simple Prep Steps

Hydrate smart. Drink water consistently the day before and the morning of your visit. Well-hydrated veins are easier to access and may reduce discomfort.

Eat a light meal. A small, balanced meal 1–2 hours before your infusion, think protein + complex carbs can minimize lightheadedness or nausea.

Limit stimulants, skip alcohol. Light coffee is usually fine; avoid heavy caffeine and skip alcohol for 24 hours before your drip.

Medications & supplements. Bring an up-to-date list of everything you take (Rx, OTC, herbs, vitamins). Mention blood thinners, diuretics, or high-dose supplements so your clinician can plan safely.

What to wear & bring. Choose loose sleeves that roll above the elbow, and bring a light layer in case you feel cool during the infusion. A book or headphones can make the time pass pleasantly. Don’t forget an ID and payment (HSA/FSA often eligible).

Plan your schedule. Most sessions take 30–60 minutes once you’re seated. Some specialty drips (like NAD+) may take longer. Add buffer time so you don’t feel rushed.

## Day-Of: What to Expect at Irving Wellness & Clinic

Check-in & consent. We’ll confirm your health history, discuss your goals, review potential ingredients, and answer any last questions. You’ll sign a consent form.

Vitals & vein assessment. We check blood pressure and heart rate and assess your hydration. A trained clinician places a small IV catheter using sterile technique.

Comfort tips. Relax your shoulders, rest your arm on a pillow, and take slow breaths. If you tend to get chilly, bring socks or a cardigan.

## Aftercare: Getting the Most from Your Drip

Hydrate & snack. Keep sipping water and enjoy a balanced meal within a few hours.

Activity. Gentle activity is fine; save high-intensity workouts for when you feel fully normal.

IV site care. Keep the small bandage on for 15–30 minutes, then remove. Mild tenderness or a small bruise can happen and typically resolves in a few days.

When to contact us. If redness, swelling, warmth, or pain increase at the IV site or you feel unwell, reach out so we can check in.

How often toI come back? Your cadence depends on goals and response. Many people start with every 2–4 weeks, then adjust. We’ll personalize a plan during your consult.

## Tailor Your Prep to Your Goals

Immune support (flu season). Prioritize sleep the night before; bring any recent illness or vaccination info.

Athletic recovery. Arrive hydrated and consider a light protein snack; avoid maximal training right after the drip.

Travel recovery/jet lag. Schedule your infusion 24–48 hours before departure or within 24 hours of landing for hydration support.

Headache/migraine prone. Eat beforehand and bring items that help you relax (eye mask, low-light sunglasses).

General wellness/energy. If you have recent labs, bringing them into context helps us fine-tune ingredients.

## FAQs

### What should I expect during my first IV therapy session?

Your first IV therapy session at our Irving clinic typically takes 30 to 60 minutes total. Expect a brief health screen, vital signs check, then placement of a small IV catheter using sterile technique. During the drip you may feel coolness in your arm or a mild taste from the vitamins. Both are normal. Trained clinicians monitor you throughout. Most patients describe the experience as calm and relaxing.

### What should I eat before IV therapy?

Have a light meal 1–2 hours before your appointment (protein + complex carbs). Avoid arriving on an empty stomach.

### Can I drink coffee before my drip?

A small coffee is usually fine. Avoid heavy caffeine and skip alcohol for 24 hours before.

### How long does an IV session take?
Plan for 30–60 minutes once seated. Some specialty drips (like NAD+) may take longer.

### Does IV therapy hurt?

You’ll feel a brief pinch when the catheter is placed. Most people relax comfortably during the infusion.

### Is there downtime afterward?

Usually none. Listen to your body and resume normal activity when you feel well.

### How often should I get IV therapy?

It depends on your goals and response. Many start every 2–4 weeks, then adjust with clinician guidance.

### Are there side effects or risks I should know about?

Mild bruising or tenderness at the IV site can happen. Serious reactions are uncommon with proper screening. Always share your medical history and current medications.
