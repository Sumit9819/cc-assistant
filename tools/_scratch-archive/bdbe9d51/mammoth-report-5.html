2. The Major Finding: a Broken URL Change
What happened. Earlier this year, between roughly April 28 and May 27, the website's addresses were changed. Product pages moved from addresses like mammothmachinery.ca/product/wl4500-wheel-loader/ to mammothmachinery.ca/wl4500-wheel-loader/. This was done before our engagement began, and it was done without forwarding the old addresses. Anyone arriving from a Google result, a bookmark, or a link on another website hit a dead page.

The scale of it

  • 29 old addresses returned a "page not found" error. Together they had earned 2,593 recorded clicks.
  • 5 more looked alive but were empty. They returned a normal page that simply read "It seems we can't find what you're looking for." These are worse than an error page, because Google keeps checking them and nothing is ever reported as broken. They carried another 1,287 clicks.
  • Combined: 3,880 clicks, about 45% of everything this website has ever earned from Google, was landing on dead pages.
  • The single biggest loss was the mini skid steer category page, which had been ranking in position 9 for "mini skid steer", the most valuable non-brand search term this business has.

All 34 addresses, and where each one now goes

Every rule below is live and was re-tested after going live. Each is a single-step forward to a working page, with no dead addresses and no redirect chains remaining.

3. The Sitemap Was Broken Too

Google had no map of the website

  • A sitemap is the file that tells Google which pages exist. The website was advertising one at mammothmachinery.ca/sitemap_index.xml, which returned an error, and the two standard backup addresses both redirected into that same error. In effect, Google had no map at all.
  • This is now working. The sitemap lists 48 addresses: all 37 pages, the 8 blog guides, and the one remaining product listing.
  • This mattered more than usual right now, because 34 brand-new forwarding rules had just been created and Google needed an efficient way to find them.
4. Specification Corrections Completed
The catalog fact-check that began in Report #4 is now finished across every product and category page. These were not cosmetic edits; customers were reading incorrect numbers.

Wheel loaders

  • TL5500: its breakout force was showing the WL7500's figure. Corrected from 127 kN to 44.8 kN. Its "Lifting Height" row contained a force measurement rather than a height; corrected to 213 inches.
  • WL7500: the specification table listed Turning Radius twice, once with the TL5500's figure. The duplicate row was removed; the catalog figure of 204 inches remains.

Mini dumpers

  • MT1350: length corrected from 81 inches to 64 inches per the catalog. Worth a second look from your side, as that is a large difference.
  • MT2850CB: the specification table and an FAQ both described an "Italian" piston pump. The catalog names no country of origin, so the unverified word was removed. The pump figures themselves are unchanged.
  • MT1750 and MT2200HL: a row displaying as "Start Method Electric / key start" was repaired to read "Start Method / Electric key start".
  • eTT900: stray pasted text was cleaned out of the Transmission label.
  • MT1350: an unverified "Italian-made hydraulic system" claim was removed from the introduction.

X-Cavator excavators, finished from Report #4

  • The 35MT correction was applied everywhere it appeared: its own page, the Mini Excavators category page, and the comparison charts on the 20MT and 27MT pages. Correct figures throughout are Kubota D1703 24.5 HP, 130.5 in dig depth, 8,488 lbs, 67 in width, 31.1 kN.
  • The 20MT and 27MT specification tables were checked line by line against catalog pages 19 and 20: every value was already correct. Only formatting was tidied, plus a hidden block of pasted text removed from both.
5. Other Work Completed

Engine manufacturer references

  • Engine names now link to a source on 13 pages: Honda on the eight dumper pages, Cummins on the three wheel loaders, and Kubota and Yanmar on the MT2850CB and 2000MT.
  • Kubota and Yanmar link to neutral reference pages rather than to their own websites, because both sell machines that compete with Mammoth. Honda and Cummins supply engines only and are linked directly.

Readability

  • Six green badges and headings on the Full Size Track Loaders page had white text on the brand green, which fails accessibility contrast standards and is genuinely hard to read. All six now use dark text on green.

Discontinued models confirmed

  • We checked the 2026 catalog for the TT660, TT1000, eTT570, eTT1000 and SST1000. None appear anywhere in it. The catalog lists 22 machines and the website has a page for every one, so the product range online is complete and correct.
  • Those five old addresses now forward to the closest current machine or category, so their remaining search traffic is not wasted.
6. What We Need From Mammoth
  • Photographs of the MTL1000 and 2000MT for their pages and category cards.
  • A named expert to credit on the blog guides. Google rewards content signed by a real, identifiable person.
  • A Terms of Sale page. The old one was retired and nothing replaced it. The existing Terms and Conditions page covers website use only and never mentions sale, purchase, refund or return. For a company selling machines up to $139,999, this is a genuine gap worth reviewing with your legal advisor.
  • Two product range questions. The tracked dumper line now starts at 1,350 lbs, where the retired TT1000 covered 1,100 lbs. The electric line now caps at 900 lbs, where the retired eTT1000 covered 1,100 lbs. If buyers still ask for those sizes, that is a product gap rather than a website one.
