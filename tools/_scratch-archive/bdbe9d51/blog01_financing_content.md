## INTRO (answer-first)
Equipment financing is a loan or lease that pays for a machine, with the machine itself acting as the security. Instead of pulling $30,000 to $140,000 out of your operating account, you spread the cost over a term that can run up to 12 years and keep your cash working on payroll, materials, and fuel. For most Canadian contractors, that is the difference between adding a machine this season and waiting three more years. Here is how the process actually works, what lenders look for, and how to walk in prepared.

## S1 — What Is Equipment Financing?
An equipment loan is a term loan used to buy a specific machine, and the machine is the collateral. If the payments stop, the lender can take the equipment back. Because the loan is secured by an asset the lender can value and resell, equipment loans are generally easier to obtain than unsecured credit for the same amount.

The alternative, paying the full price in cash, feels cheaper because there is no interest. But it concentrates risk in the worst place: your operating account.

One slow season plus one big repair on a machine you drained your cash for, and suddenly payroll is the problem. Financing exists to keep that risk off your books.

## S2 — Loan or Lease: What Is the Difference?
With a loan, you own the machine from day one and build equity with every payment. With a lease, the financing company owns it and you pay for the use, usually with a buyout option at the end.

TABLE (container-grid, 4 rows × 3 cols):
Header: (blank) | Equipment loan | Lease
Ownership: Yours from day one | Lessor's until buyout
Upfront cost: Down payment usually required | Often little or none
Payments: Principal + interest | Use-based; may be deductible as a business expense
End of term: You own it outright | Return, renew, or buy out

Lease payments may be deductible as a business expense; loan interest and depreciation follow different tax treatment. Which is better depends on your revenue, taxes, and how long you keep machines. Talk to your accountant before choosing; the right answer is different for a landscaper replacing dumpers every four years than for an excavation company running a wheel loader for a decade.

## S3 — What Do Lenders Actually Look At?
Approval is not a mystery. Lenders evaluate a short, predictable list:

CHECKLIST (icon-list / check items):
✓ Company history, operations, and management experience
✓ Financial statements, typically the last two years
✓ Monthly cash-flow forecasts for the rest of this year plus the next 12 months
✓ Debt-to-equity ratio and working capital
✓ How the machine will increase sales or cut costs, stated specifically

"Bankers need numbers."

The strongest application is not a perfect credit history. It is a specific story with figures attached: this machine lets us take on two more retaining-wall jobs a month at this margin, and here is the cash-flow forecast that shows it.

## S4 — What Can Financing Actually Cover?
More than the sticker price. BDC, Canada's bank for entrepreneurs, can finance up to 125% of the upfront cost of equipment, so transportation, shipping, installation, and operator training can ride along in the same loan. Terms on equipment loans run up to 12 years, principal postponement is available for up to the first two years on some loans, and repayment can be structured seasonally: smaller payments through the winter, larger ones in your busy months. For Canadian landscapers and excavation contractors whose revenue arrives between April and November, that seasonal structure is often the single most valuable feature.

## S5 — How to Apply: Step by Step
NUMBERED LIST:
1. Get a written quote for the machine, with delivery and attachments itemized.
2. Pull your last two years of financial statements.
3. Build a monthly cash-flow forecast covering the rest of this year plus 12 months.
4. Write down, in two or three sentences, how the machine increases revenue or cuts costs.
5. Apply through your lender, and compare at least two offers on rate, term, and flexibility.
6. Close, take delivery, and register your warranty.

## FAQ (4 items, accordion or sibling FAQ pattern)
Q1: Can I finance used or already-purchased equipment?
A1: Often, yes. BDC notes that even previously purchased equipment can be financed, which lets you refinance a recent cash purchase to put working capital back in the business. Policies vary by lender, so ask before assuming.

Q2: How long are equipment loan terms in Canada?
A2: Equipment loans commonly run up to 12 years, with the machine as collateral. Longer terms mean lower payments but more total interest, so match the term to how long the machine will earn for you.

Q3: Is leasing or buying better for taxes?
A3: It depends on your situation. Lease payments may be deductible as a business expense, while ownership follows depreciation rules. The difference is real money either way, so have your accountant run both before you sign.

Q4: Can a newer business get equipment financing?
A4: It is harder without two years of statements, but not impossible, because the machine secures the loan. Expect a larger down payment and be ready with a detailed cash-flow forecast that shows exactly how the equipment pays for itself.
