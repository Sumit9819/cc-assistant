3. The rules that decide whether it earns

Read on the monetisation policy page today. The policy formerly called "repetitious content" is now "inauthentic content"; YouTube's July 2026 guidance split it into three named categories.

Not monetisable: "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"; "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative". Allowed: "the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value", with the "same intro and outro" fine when "the bulk of your content is different". Also restricted: "AI-generated personas to deliver information on sensitive topics" such as health, legal or financial advice. (YouTube Help 1311392)

How the studio stands against each line: every video is written from primary .gov sources with its own argument and its own cut; the originality gate blocks repeated clips and structural sameness; no AI persona gives advice on health, finance, legal or political matters, which is a standing rule here. The one line to keep watching is "generic templates giving the impression of mass production": the device library is exactly a set of templates, and what keeps a video out of that category is that the substance, the footage and the documents differ every time. The card-versus-voice check added today guards the other half: nothing on screen that the voice does not say.

4. Uploading by hand or by API

Version 1 ends at the files and you upload by hand, and that is still the right call. Read on Google's own pages today:

"All videos uploaded via the videos.insert endpoint from unverified API projects created after 28 July 2020 will be restricted to private viewing mode." Lifting it means the project must "undergo an audit to verify compliance with the Terms of Service." Quota: "A call to this method has a quota cost of 1 unit in the Video Uploads quota bucket." Default allocation: "100 search.list calls, 100 videos.insert calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints." (Google Developers: videos.insert; getting-started)

Partner Program thresholds, read today on YouTube Help: "Get 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months" or "Get 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". (YouTube Help 72851)

Recommendation: keep manual upload. If an uploader is ever wanted, it starts with the audit form, not with code.

5. Before the first upload, once

  1. Verify the channel with a phone number in Studio: unlocks custom thumbnails, videos over 15 minutes and live features.
  2. Decide the channel's category and default language once, and set the default upload visibility to Private so a video can be checked on YouTube's own player before it goes public.
  3. Read the Partner Program thresholds on the day you apply; they change, and the page above is the authority.
  4. Keep the credits block the studio writes into every description: it is what the music and stills licences require.
