Why the usual power words are the wrong list

SHOCKING promises intensity. PAYCHECK names something the viewer owns. The second one wins every time, and it is not close — the pull is in the possession, not the volume.

Ownership

Converts a policy story into the viewer’s own story before the click. The reason $600 EVERYONE works: everyone has sold something.

A removal outranks an addition of the same size — that is loss aversion, and it is the most reliable lever on the list. FROZEN and CRUSHED both live here.

LEGAL is the single strongest word available to this channel. It reframes an outrage as a rule, and the viewer clicks to find out how that can possibly be true.

The house style: the rule says one thing and does another. Underused by everyone else, and it is exactly what the redact layout was built to draw.

Two figures still need a source. $7.25 (since July 2009), 3.69 cents per penny (US Mint FY2024), 91 percent (top statutory rate, 1954–63) and the Sixteenth Amendment’s thirty words are all locked. 690,000 and the 1099-K delay history are not — NHTSA’s final Cash for Clunkers count is nearer 677,000, and I will not put a number on screen I cannot cite. Both are marked above and neither ships until checked. The photographs are generated placeholders for the layout test; the shipped versions use public-domain federal archive material.

Two things to rate. First the words — tell me which of the four you would put on the wage video, and whether the SHOCKING control reads as badly to you as it does to me. Then the layouts, by letter. My order is E collide, then D verdict, then F redact: E because it tells the whole story with no caption at all, D because it is the fastest read in the set, and F because the cream ground is the only thing here that does not disappear into a dark feed. G stamp I would keep for programmes that ended — VOID, EXPIRED, DENIED are all waiting for the right story.
