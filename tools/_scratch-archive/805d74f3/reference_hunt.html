The static half — where Pinterest earns its keep

Ranked by leverage. The first two would change more than the other four combined.

Thumbnails

highest leverage

Everything upstream is wasted if nobody clicks. We have a working build process — generate the image, burn the text locally, three A/B candidates — but no visual system: no fixed type treatment, no repeatable composition, nothing that makes two of our videos look like the same channel.

The motion half — look somewhere else

Pinterest is stills. Timing, easing and transitions cannot be judged from a frozen frame, and these are exactly where our system is most invented rather than benchmarked.

What we most need motion reference for

  • Section transitions. We have precisely one: a dip to black. That is the most basic transition that exists.
  • Stat card animation. Count-up plus a rule that draws. Functional, generic.
  • Caption animation. Just rebuilt with a word-by-word gold highlight, never compared against anything.

Where to actually look

  • Dribbble — hosts looping video previews, so motion is visible. Search motion graphics transition, kinetic typography.
  • Real videos in the niche. The most honest reference. When something makes you re-watch it, note the timestamp and send me the link plus the time — that beats any mood board.

Do not spend time on

  • Subtitle styling — rebuilt today: studio face at 124px, word-by-word gold, cues that break on sentences.
  • Specimen and mechanism imagery — Gemini covers it, 16:9, better than any stock.
  • Intro and end cards — built, though a strong reference would still be welcome for the end card, which is untested.
