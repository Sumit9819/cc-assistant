What I got wrong. I optimised for legibility at small size and treated the photograph as a cost to remove — I even wrote up “needs no photograph” as a feature. A thumbnail’s job is to put a picture in someone’s head, and type alone cannot do that. The research puts it in one line: a face conveys emotion, so a faceless thumbnail must convey an event. My cards conveyed a fact. A number is not an object, and a fact is not an event.

What the research actually says

I went and read it properly rather than reasoning from first principles, which is what produced the type-only mistake.

69% / 80%
of breakout videos carry a face

Rising to 80 percent among the biggest overperformers, and an expressive one is worth roughly 20–30% CTR. We cannot use your face — so the substitute has to be a recognisable object and a visible event.

120–150px
the real viewing size

On a phone, where 70%+ of views happen. I had been judging at 210, which was generous by nearly half. Every sheet below leads at 128.

≤ 3 words
on the main line

Under four words tests about 30% better than text-heavy designs. The title carries the explanation; the thumbnail carries the proof. Never mirror the title.

3 layers
ground, subject, type — overlapping

The subject crosses in front of the headline. That single move is what reads as depth, and it is what every one of your references does.

The four, full size

One system, four grounds. Every subject is a real photograph under a licence we can use — a coin, a dome, a scrapyard, a federal building.

Where the pictures come from now. Not generated. US federal works are public domain outright, and Wikimedia’s Commons search was already wired into the pipeline — it returned the 2025 Lincoln cent at 2000×2000, the Capitol, the IRS building and a real scrapyard. A new cutout module keys the subject with a flood fill rather than a neural matte, because archival and product photography is shot against flat grounds or open sky and keys cleanly. Anything that will not key becomes an angled card instead. 690,000 still needs checking — NHTSA’s final count is nearer 677,000.
