The search work is landing. These pages rank on page one of Google for exactly what the business sells. What stops them earning is on the shop side, not the marketing side: one range has no products, one has no stock, and one has no prices. This brief covers the third, which is new, and shows why we think the three are the same problem.

01

New: soil, gravel and mulch show no prices at all

Every one of the 12 products in the soil, aggregates and mulch ranges displays no price. In place of one, the page reads “Available in-store!”. Google is also given a price of zero in the page’s product data, which means these products cannot show a price in search results either.

These pages rank on the first page of Google for the searches that matter most locally:

Across the whole range that is 3,400 searches and 13 clicks in 90 days. Soil and gravel are commodities: the shopper comparing three local yards is comparing price per yard and delivery cost. A listing that shows neither is the one they skip, however high it ranks.

Worth noting: the page copy here is not the problem. The soil page already carries a full description covering topsoil, triple mix, top dressing and local delivery. We have checked it. Adding more words will not fix a missing price.

The decision

Can these 12 products carry a price on the website, even a “from” price per cubic yard?

If yes

The rankings are already earned. Prices and a delivery cost turn first-page listings into quotes and orders, and let the products appear with a price in Google.

If prices genuinely vary

Then a visible “from” price with a delivery calculator or a clear quote button still beats “Available in-store!”, which reads as unavailable.

02

The same shape, three times

Taken separately these look like three unrelated issues. Side by side they are one:

Nearly 29,000 searches reached these pages in 90 days and 205 people clicked. Every one of them ranks where a business wants to rank. None of them can complete a sale, and no amount of further search work changes that.

The three are not equally hard. Bike storage needs a buying decision. Pond livestock is seasonal and largely fixes itself in spring, provided the pages are handled properly in the meantime. Prices are the most straightforward of the three and cover the range that ranks best.

03

What we are asking, in order

#QuestionWho can answer it
1Can soil, gravel and mulch show prices, or a “from” price per yard?Straightforward, and the largest quick win
2Is Trimetals bike storage coming back, or a comparable line?A purchasing decision, covered in the previous brief
3When do pond plants and fish return, so the pages can say so?Seasonal; a date is enough

Any answer is workable, including no. Knowing which of the three are permanent and which are seasonal lets us handle each page correctly rather than leaving first-page rankings pointed at things nobody can buy.
