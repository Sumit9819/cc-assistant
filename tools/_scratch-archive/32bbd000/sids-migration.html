2 Why move

The honest case

Let me be straight about something first, because it affects how you judge the rest. Elementor is not magically better than Divi. Both are page builders, both store your design in their own format, and both are a commitment. Swapping one for the other is not, by itself, a win.

The real value is the clean rebuild. The current site has picked up four years of layers, and a fresh start is the only realistic way to shed them. Here is what is actually sitting on the site today, all of it measured rather than guessed:

The point

Every one of these could in principle be fixed on the current site. In practice they have accumulated because fixing them one at a time on a live store is slow and risky. A rebuild fixes them all at once, on a staging site, where nothing can break for a customer.

3 The theme question

Hello Elementor, with a child theme

A WordPress theme controls the parts of the site that are not page content. There are two sensible routes, and one of them is clearly right here.

Recommendation

Hello Elementor, plus a small child theme

A custom theme only makes sense if you are not using a page builder. If you build a custom theme and then run Elementor on top of it, you pay for both and get the benefits of neither.

The child theme is a small addition on top of Hello. It is where any custom code lives, so that updates to Hello never wipe it. It is an hour of setup and it saves real trouble later.

One alternative, named honestly

There is a third route: a modern block theme with no page builder at all. It is the lightest option and it is where WordPress itself is heading. I am not recommending it here, because it is harder to edit without training and you have already chosen Elementor. But you should know it existed as a choice rather than find out later.

4 Compatibility

What happens to your 59 plugins

This is usually the scariest part of a rebuild, and here it is genuinely reassuring. Almost nothing you depend on is tied to Divi. Payments, shipping, discounts, email, reviews, search, tax and SEO are all theme-independent and simply come across.

The reassuring part

The SEO work is safe

Every page title, meta description and category write-up we have produced lives in Rank Math and in WordPress’s own category records. None of it is stored inside Divi. It all comes across exactly as it is, and so do the product listings, reviews, order history and customer accounts.

5 The plugin audit

What is actually being used

Rather than asking you which plugins matter, I read the stored settings of each one and checked eighteen pages of the live site for its output. That changed the answer substantially, and it changed it away from an aggressive cut.

Correction to my earlier read

Four plugins I had marked as removable are load-bearing

My first pass tested four pages and concluded several plugins were idle. That method was too weak, and it produced false negatives in four different ways: a plugin deliberately hidden on the pages I sampled, a page that redirects when the basket is empty, a feature scoped to one product category, and scripts held back until a visitor accepts cookies. Two things worth fixing rather than removing

Zero product reviews, on all 322 products

I checked ten products across a busy category: no reviews, no star ratings, and the review form is not rendering at all. Customer Reviews for WooCommerce is installed and doing nothing visible. That is not really a plugin problem, it is a missed opportunity, because review stars in Google results lift click rates markedly. The rebuild is the moment to switch reviews back on and turn the request emails on properly.

ClearSale is running with debug mode enabled in production. Harmless, but it writes log files nobody reads. Worth turning off during the move.

Where the weight actually goes

The speed gain does not come from the plugin count. It comes from the specific things being removed: 13 of the homepage’s 52 style and script files belong to the four Divi add-ons, four more come from duplicated tracking, and the 110KB icon font and 32KB of unused styling go with them. Keeping ClearSale, CURCY and Fluid Checkout costs almost nothing by comparison, and each one protects something that earns money.

6 Protecting the rankings

The one rule that matters most

Sid’s currently earns around 100,000 non-brand impressions a quarter and holds page-one positions on genuinely valuable terms. A careless rebuild can lose that in a fortnight, and it is slow to win back. Almost all of that risk comes down to one thing: web addresses must not change.

Identical, deliberately. Including the slightly awkward ones like /outdoor-lighting-2/. A tidier address is not worth a ranking.

Alongside that, four checks run before anything goes live:

  • Every address answered. All 511 are requested on the new site and must return a page, not an error.
  • Every page title and description matched against the old site, one by one.
  • Structured data preserved, so products keep their prices and stock status in search results.
  • A full backup of the live site kept untouched for 30 days after launch, so reverting is always possible.

7 How the move happens

Six phases, and the shop never closes

The new site is built on a private staging address that customers never see. The live store keeps trading normally throughout. Only the final phase touches it, and that step is measured in minutes.

1

Copy and freeze

Week 1

A complete copy of the live site is taken to staging. Every custom code snippet is read and catalogued. We agree exactly which plugins are retiring. Nothing is designed yet.

Live site unaffected
2

Clean foundation

Week 1–2

Fresh WordPress, Hello Elementor and its child theme, Elementor Pro, WooCommerce, and only the plugins from the keep list. The Divi add-ons come off. Products, categories, orders, customers, images and all SEO settings are imported.

Live site unaffected
3

Build the templates

Week 2–3

The pieces that appear everywhere: header, footer, product page, category listing, blog article, cart and checkout. Getting these right is most of the work, because 500 of the 511 addresses are drawn by them.

Live site unaffected
4

Rebuild the eight pages

Week 3–4

Home, About, Contact, Delivery, Hardscaping, Outdoor Lighting, Partners, Blog. Rebuilt to match the current design unless you want changes. The 22 blog articles are simple stacks of text and pictures, so they convert almost mechanically rather than needing redesign.

Live site unaffected
5

Check everything

Week 4–5

All 511 addresses tested. Titles and descriptions compared against the old site. A real test purchase put through end to end, including payment, tax, shipping and the invoice email. Speed and accessibility measured so we can prove the improvement rather than claim it.

Live site unaffected
6

Switch over

One evening

Done outside trading hours. Orders paused for roughly thirty minutes, the new site takes over, the final orders are carried across, then a live re-test. Google is told to re-read the site the same night. The old site is kept, intact, for 30 days.

8 What you get

Where this actually pays off

GainWhy it follows from the rebuild
A faster storeFive Divi add-ons, a 110KB icon font, 32KB of unused styling and two duplicate tracking scripts all leave at once. The font loading that causes the page to jump gets fixed properly rather than patched.
Google shopping listings finally runningThe product feed is rebuilt from scratch as part of the move. Where these listings already appear for Sid’s they earn roughly six times the clicks of ordinary search results. This is the biggest single opportunity on the site and it is currently switched off by a broken feed.
You can edit your own siteElementor is materially easier to hand over than Divi with five add-ons layered on it. Fewer moving parts, one visual editor, and templates that behave predictably.
Accessible and zoomablePinch-zoom comes back. The known contrast and empty-link problems get fixed in the build rather than retrofitted.
Fewer things to breakAround 19 plugins removed, and with them 13 of the homepage’s 52 style and script files. Every plugin removed is one fewer update to track and one fewer thing that can take the store offline on a Friday night.
What this does not fix

A rebuild does not sell anything on its own. The two biggest commercial problems on the site are still inventory: 21,000 searches a quarter for bike storage with no bike products in stock, and hornwort ranking fifth while out of stock. Those need decisions from you, and they are worth more than everything in this document. The rebuild is the right moment to settle them, not a substitute for doing so.
