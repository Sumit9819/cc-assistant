The headline this sprint: the Outdoor Lighting page was carrying a technical fault that kept it out of the site’s own index file — the file that tells Google which pages exist. It has been fixed and the page is back in. Alongside it, three product category pages were rewritten around real customer searches, four links were added between guides and the products they discuss, and three store-page faults were cleared.

01

Outdoor Lighting: A Page Google Could Not Properly See

The Outdoor Lighting page told Google that its master copy lived at a different web address — but that address redirected straight back to the page itself. A circular instruction. The visible consequence: the page was missing from the sitemap, so Google was never being told it existed.

What was doneVerified result
Circular address instruction corrected so the page points to itselfRe-read from the live page — correct, no faults reported
Page restored to the sitemapSitemap grew from 14 to 15 pages and now lists it
Page wording matched to what people search — it ranked for phrases that appeared nowhere in its text“landscape lighting”, “path lighting”, “energy-efficient”, “installation” and “cost” all went from 0 mentions to present
Two new questions answered on the page: does Sid’s install lighting, and what does it costFAQ went from 5 to 7 questions; layout unchanged
Link added from the fall maintenance guideLive — the page previously had only two links pointing to it from the site’s own content

The installation answer states plainly that Sid’s supplies the fixtures rather than installing them, and offers planning help at the yard instead. Before writing it we checked the home, about, hardscaping, delivery and contact pages to confirm the business does not advertise installation anywhere. The cost answer explains what drives the price and points to live per-fixture pricing rather than quoting a figure.

Why it matters: this page is already Google’s preferred page on the site for seven local lighting searches — 1,337 appearances over 90 days — but sits at positions 20 to 50, where almost nobody clicks. It was competing with one hand tied.

AI Search: Who Is Reading the Site

Search is splitting into two channels: classic Google results, and AI assistants that answer buyers directly. The site now logs every AI assistant that reads it. Here is the first full picture, 30 days to Aug 19:

Who is readingMeta AI 423 · ChatGPT 18 · You.com 8 · Amazon 1 · OpenAI Search 1
Not yet reading the siteClaude, Perplexity and Google’s AI crawler recorded zero visits this period
What they readMostly the checkout page (114 visits) and filtered product listings — the same range seen through different sorting and currency options — rather than the guides and category pages

Why it matters: AI assistants cite sources that answer questions with real, specific information. Almost all of the current AI activity is one system reading transactional pages that say nothing about the business. The category rewrites and the lighting page work in this report are exactly the kind of content that becomes citable — plain answers, named brands, real locations, honest limits.
