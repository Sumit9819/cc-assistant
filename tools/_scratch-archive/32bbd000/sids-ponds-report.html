The Outdoor Lighting page

Technical fix + content

The Outdoor Lighting page carried a technical fault that was quietly keeping it out of Google’s index of the site. That has been corrected, and the page has been brought in line with what people are actually searching for.

What was wrong

The page told Google that its master copy lived at a different web address — but that address redirected straight back to the page itself. A circular instruction.

The visible consequence: the page was missing from the site’s sitemap, the file that tells Google which pages exist. It has now been added.

It ranked for phrases it never actually said

The page appears in Google results for a set of local lighting searches, yet several of those exact phrases appeared nowhere in its text. The wording has been corrected so the page now says what it is being found for — without changing the layout or adding new sections.

Two questions added, answered honestly

People search for lighting installation and lighting cost in Mississauga. The page now answers both. The installation answer states plainly that Sid’s supplies the fixtures rather than installing them, and points the reader toward planning help at the yard. The cost answer explains what drives the price and links to the live per-fixture pricing rather than quoting a figure.

What the pattern shows. Activity is heavily concentrated in one reader, and it is not reading the parts of the site that describe the business. Of its visits, 114 landed on the checkout page, and most of the rest went to filtered and paginated product listings — the same product range viewed through different sorting and currency options — rather than the guides, category descriptions and landing pages.

The readers most associated with answering shopping questions in AI assistants — Claude, Perplexity and Google’s AI crawler — recorded no visits in this period. The content work described in this report is the groundwork that makes a site worth citing when they do arrive: clear category descriptions, real answers to real questions, and pages that state plainly what the business sells and where.
