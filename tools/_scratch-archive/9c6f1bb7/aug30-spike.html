Search Console Forensics irvingwellnessclinic.com Reviewed 3 Sep 2026

The August 30 Impression Spike

On 30 August the property logged 65,572 impressions against a 29-day average of 1,156, and average position collapsed from ~30 to near 1. Clicks did not move. The number is really in Google's data — and it is a counting fault, not a ranking or indexing event. 94% of it was attributed to a URL Google has never crawled, on a surface that has been reporting normally for months.

Verdict

No web-search ranking changed and nothing was indexed. 61,964 of the 65,572 impressions landed on /?utm_source=gmb&utm_medium=gmb — the tracking URL in the Business Profile listing — across four “near me” queries at position 1.00. That URL routinely appears in Search Console because it is the link served in the local pack; that part is normal and months old. What is not normal is the volume: one day produced 19.5× that URL's entire recorded lifetime, and its click-through rate collapsed from its own 1.6% baseline to 0.0016%. Decisively, the Business Profile performance report shows no matching spike — so those impressions were almost certainly never served to anyone. This is a Search Console logging defect: not a ranking event, not an indexing event, and not real exposure.

04Why this URL is in Search Console at all

This is the part that looks wrong but isn't. A URL does not have to be indexed to collect impressions, because indexing and Search-performance reporting are two separate systems.

When someone searches a local query and Google shows the map pack, the “Website” link on your listing points at whatever URL is configured in the Business Profile — in your case the UTM-tagged one. Search Console attributes that appearance to the URL that was shown. It never asks whether that URL is in the web index. No crawl, no canonical, no sitemap entry is involved.

The position it reports is the position of the pack block on the page, which is why it reads as a flat 1.00 rather than fluctuating the way a competitive organic rank does.

You can see this plainly in what that URL has collected over its whole life. It is a local-pack query profile, not a web-index one:

05Evidence chain

Six independent checks. Each one on its own is suggestive; together they close the question.

  1. E1

    The volume is physically impossible. recovery iv near me collected 31 impressions across its entire recorded life and then 18,159 in one day. The URL as a whole holds 3,192 lifetime impressions against 62,126 on 30 August. The day is 58% of everything the whole property recorded across the other 293 days on file. No demand event does that to a long-tail phrase in one town. Warehouse, 2025-11-11 → 2026-09-01, 295 dates

  2. E2

    CTR collapsed against the URL's own baseline. This is the right control, not a site-wide average: on all other days that URL converts at 1.6% (51 clicks / 3,192 impressions). At its own normal rate, 62,126 impressions should have produced roughly 990 clicks. It produced 1 — a rate of 0.0016%, a thousandfold collapse on the same surface, same listing, same week.   3. E3

    Nothing was indexed. URL Inspection on https://irvingwellnessclinic.com/?utm_source=gmb&utm_medium=gmb returns coverage state “URL is unknown to Google”, verdict NEUTRAL, last crawl time null, no referring URLs, in no sitemap. Had a bug pushed this URL into the index, the inspection would show a coverage state and a Google-selected canonical.   4. E4

    Rank did not move — only counts did. The identical queries resolve to /iv-therapy-irving-tx/ at positions 22.9–37.1, exactly where they sat before and after. Those organic rows also rose in volume (4 impressions on the 29th → 385 on the 30th → 16 on the 31st), so the over-count reached the organic surface too. Position was untouched throughout; only impression counts inflated. Section 03 table; warehouse, 2026-08-24 → 2026-08-31

  5. E5

    The average-position “improvement” is arithmetic. Impression-weighted position for the whole property: 29.94 on 29 Aug → 2.41 on 30 Aug → 24.93 on 31 Aug.   6. E6

    It self-corrected in 24 hours. That URL logs position-1 “near me” rows most weeks at ordinary scale — 18 impressions on 31 Jul, 19 on 4 Aug, 138 on 21 Aug, 99 on 23 Aug. On 30 Aug the same signature logged 61,964. The next day: 23. A ranking gain does not evaporate overnight; a bad counting run does. Warehouse, page LIKE '%utm_source=gmb%' AND query LIKE '%near me%'

  7. E7

    The Business Profile shows no spike at all. Operator-verified: the GBP performance report for the same period is flat. This is the control that closes the case. If the pack had genuinely been served 62,126 times in a day, the listing's own views would have surged in step. They did not — so the impressions were very likely never shown to anyone. What it is not

This is not the 2025–26 Google impression-inflation defect. That one ran 13 May 2025 to 27 April 2026 and was corrected without restating history; 30 August 2026 falls four months clear of it. It is also not a display error in the interface — the raw Search Analytics API returns the same 65,572, so the bad number is in Google's stored data, not in the rendering of it. And it is not an indexing incident: E3 shows the URL's index status is unchanged and empty on both sides of the event.

What remains is a logging or attribution fault in the impression-counting pipeline on that date, weighted overwhelmingly toward the local-pack surface. Google does not publish an incident feed for this, so the fault is diagnosed from its signature rather than confirmed from an announcement.

07Confidence

Separating what the data proves from what it strongly implies.

Verified

  • The 65,572 figure is in Google's own API, not a UI artefact.
  • 61,964 impressions (94.5%) attribute to the utm_source=gmb homepage URL on four queries.
  • That URL is unknown to Google's index, has never been crawled, and its index status is unchanged either side of the event.
  • That URL has been collecting impressions normally for months (3,192 across 32 active days, 1.6% CTR) — its presence in reporting is not new and not a fault.
  • The volume on 30 August exceeds any plausible demand: 58% of the property's other 293 days combined, and 586× one query's lifetime total.
  • The Business Profile performance report shows no corresponding spike (operator-verified), so no matching real-world exposure occurred.
  • No page's position changed. Clicks, traffic and leads were unaffected.

Inferred, not proven

  • That the affected reporting channel is the Business Profile / local-pack surface. The reasoning: the URL is the one configured in the GBP listing, its lifetime query profile is unmistakably pack-shaped (google reviewed wellness clinic, brand terms at 12–27% CTR against 0% on broad terms), and an uncrawled URL has no other route into Search-performance data. Note this is now a claim about which row the bad count was written to, not about anything real being served.
  • Why it happened on that particular Sunday is unknown, and Google publishes no incident record to check against.
  • Whether Google will retroactively correct the stored figure. The 2025–26 inflation defect was fixed without restating history, so the working assumption is that 30 August stays wrong in the interface permanently.

08What to do

  1. Change nothing on the site, and leave the UTM URL exactly as it is. It is doing its job, it is not indexed, it is not a duplicate-content risk, and it never became one — E3 confirms Google has still never crawled it. Removing or rewriting it would cost you the local-pack attribution you built it for and would not have prevented this.
  2. Do not report this as growth. If 30 August lands inside a client reporting window it will inflate impressions by roughly 57× and show average position improving from ~30 to ~2. Both are artefacts. Report the month on clicks, and on impressions with the tracking URL excluded.
  3. Re-run the Business Profile performance pull when the API quota resets, to confirm the local surface as the source and close the one inferred leg of this report.
  4. Follow up on /reverse-insulin-resistance/. Position 2.6 on “insulin resistance” with 653 impressions and zero clicks over two days is the real story of that weekend. Check whether an AI Overview is taking the answer before treating it as a title or snippet problem.
  5. Set an alert threshold, not a fix. This signature will recur at small volume most weeks. A day is worth investigating only when impressions on real pages move — the tracking URL's numbers should be excluded from monitoring by default.
